Brand proposal · four directions

The Veredictum Seal

vere dictum — truly spoken

A certification mark in the openEHR community's own colours (teal #258BB0, signal orange #FF861C, read off openehr.org). Every direction keeps the same anatomy — ring wording, the V that doubles as a checkmark, the Latin motto — so the choice is about voice, not structure. All shown on light and dark grounds.

The four directions

A: the working favourite. B inverts it into an ink stamp. C is the banded quality-mark shape people know from inspection seals. D is the formal notary.

A

Signal

Deep-teal ground, white verdict stroke with the orange core, knurled orange edge. Reads as an official mark at full size and survives as a plain icon when the ring drops away.

B

Stamp

The rubber-stamp read: transparent ground, inked teal linework, the verdict stroke in orange. Strongest on the certificate itself — it looks pressed onto the page. Needs a theme-aware line colour on dark grounds.

C

Quality mark

The banded inspection-mark silhouette (the shape KEMA-style marks trained everyone to trust). Flat, modern, loudest of the four at small sizes. The band takes the page ground, so it punches a hole through whatever it sits on.

D

Notary

Near-navy ink, everything struck in orange and old gold — wax-seal formality. The most ceremonial; the least openEHR-teal of the four.

The seal card

The certificate a verified deployment displays — and the 1280×640 social-preview master. The verdict pipeline fills the three dotted slots (product, profile, date); the file ships them empty. Shown with direction A.

One caution, stated before it bites: the palette honours the openEHR community and the ring names openEHR only descriptively (what is being tested). The mark must never read as a Foundation endorsement — the brand README carries that line, and the seal never appears beside the Foundation's own logo without their blessing. Worth raising in the collaboration conversation already on the table.
